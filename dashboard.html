F1 race predictor

A machine-learning model that predicts the finishing order of a Formula 1 race, plus each driver's chance of winning and of making the podium. Tested on 88 real races (2023 to round 18 of 2026), where each season was predicted using only the seasons before it.

Honest summary. After qualifying, the starting grid already explains most of the result, so the order predictions are only a little better than "they finish where they start". The measurable improvement of this version is in the probabilities: 2 to 6% lower error than the original, so "30% to win" is a slightly more trustworthy number. Randomness (safety cars, first-lap crashes, weather) is not modelled. Not betting advice.

1. How often is it right? (after qualifying)

Four ways of scoring the same predictions. Grey is the simple "finish in grid order" rule, orange is the original model, blue is the new one. Differences in winner calls are within the noise of 88 races.

2. And before qualifying?

Before qualifying there is no grid to lean on, so the model only has recent form to go on, which makes it much weaker (grey is "recent driver form" alone).

3. Can you trust the percentages?

Log loss measures how far the probabilities were from what happened (lower is better).

4. What does the model look at?

Qualifying position dominates once it is known. Before qualifying, a driver's recent grid slots and points share matter most.
